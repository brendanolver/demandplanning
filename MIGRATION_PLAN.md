# Migrate Units Sold & SOH from Shopify export to Apparel Magic API

**Status: planned, not started.** Parked until Brendan is back from holiday and has time to watch the migration closely — do not begin implementation without his explicit go-ahead.

## Context

Today the app's stock-on-hand and units-sold numbers come from two CSVs (`shopify_inventory.csv`, `shopify_sales.csv`) regenerated by the Pipeline server roughly every 3 hours during working hours. Brendan confirmed Apparel Magic (AM) is actually the *upstream* source of truth — AM pushes stock to Shopify, and every Shopify order syncs back into AM in near real time as a sales order under customer "ONLINE SALES" (account 2264) or "ICONIC ONLINE SALES" (account 2356). Going to AM directly for these two numbers moves the app closer to the source, not further from it, and can be refreshed far more often than the 3-hour CSV cycle.

Live API research this session confirmed both feeds exist and are fast enough:
- **Stock**: AM's `/inventory` endpoint returns per-SKU-per-size records (`sku_alt`, `qty_avail_sell`) at ~1–1.5s per 100 rows — a full catalogue pull is ~5–7 minutes.
- **Sales**: AM's `/orders` endpoint, filtered by `customer_name` + `date=`, returns real per-line-item sales with a `shopify_id` linking back to the actual order. A full day (113 orders) came back in 2 quick pages — cheap enough to poll incrementally rather than rebuild history each time.

**Decisions locked in with Brendan:**
- Sales count at **order date**, matching current Shopify "Quantity ordered" semantics — no change to what velocity/sell-through/reorder math *means*.
- **Iconic Online Sales is blended into the same sold totals** as Online Sales (not tracked separately, not excluded) — but its 25% marketplace commission is deducted from Price/Sell Value/Margin% for those specific sales (see Phase 2 "Iconic marketplace commission" below); units-sold counts are unaffected either way.
- **Sales history is hybrid**: existing 365-day history stays as-is (still Shopify-CSV-sourced); from the cutover point forward, new days are AM-sourced and layered on top. No slow one-time backfill.
- **Refresh stays on-demand** — page load / clicking ↻ Refresh, matching every other data source in the app today. No new background polling infrastructure.

## Scope boundary — what's replaced vs kept

Traced every consumer of `shopifyInvData` / `shopifySalesData` in `index.html` to confirm this is a surgical, two-touch-point swap:

- **Replaced:** `sohMap` construction in `processRows()` (currently `row['Ending inventory units']`, index.html:7890-7893) and the `qty`/`salesBySkuDay` accumulation in `buildSalesMaps()` (index.html:7327-7349).
- **Kept unchanged:** everything else that reads `shopifyInvData`/`shopifySalesData` is product *metadata* (name, colour, size, category, price, RRP, vendor, status) — `priceMap` construction in `buildSalesMaps()`, the `invSkuMap` fallback in `buildAMDataFromAPI()`, and the price-fill loops. None of that needs to change; it's not what's being asked to move.
- **Also kept unchanged:** the existing AM on-order/CORE/launch-date logic (`buildAMDataFromAPI`, `syncAMMidCodes`, `styleFromSku`, `processRows`' AM-matching block) — already AM-sourced, unaffected.
- **Design principle throughout: AM data is preferred when available, with the existing CSV-derived value as fallback** — not a hard cutover. If AM is slow, down, or missing a SKU, the app degrades to today's behaviour rather than showing wrong/zero numbers.

## Phase 1 — Stock on hand (do this first: simplest, no time-series merging, immediate live-ness win)

3. In `processRows()`, change the `stock` assignment (around index.html:7893) to `window.amStockMap?.[sku] ?? sohMap[sku] ?? 0` — AM preferred, CSV fallback.
4. Hook the sync into `startAutoSync()` (index.html:6792) alongside the existing `syncAMMidCodes(true)` call, and into the manual ↻ Refresh path — on-demand only, no timer.

## Phase 2 — Units sold + real per-unit sell price (after Phase 1 is verified and shipped)

1. New function `syncAMSales()`: fetches `/.netlify/functions/am-proxy?path=orders&customer_name=ONLINE+SALES&date=<today>` and the same for `customer_name=ICONIC+ONLINE+SALES`, for today plus a small trailing window (recommend last 3–7 days, to cover any gap since the last refresh) — not a full history pull.
2. For each `order_items` line: resolve the real Shopify SKU from `style_number` + `size` (reconstruct the same way the app's own SKUs are formatted — verify against a real sample before trusting it), skip known non-product line items (`REDORETURNS`, `ORDERPROTECTION`, and scan a broader order sample for any other service/fee style_numbers before finalizing the exclusion list — mirrors the existing `excludedDraftCats` pattern already used for the INSURANCE category).
3. Merge Online Sales + Iconic Online Sales qty together per SKU/day (blended, per decision).
4. **Date key — verified exact match, no conversion needed:** the Shopify CSV's "Day" column is ISO (`2025-05-11`, confirmed from a real sample). AM's order object carries `date_internal` in the *same* ISO format (`2026-08-03`, confirmed from a real order) alongside its display-format `date` (`08/03/2026`) — use `date_internal` directly as the merge key. Avoids any US MM/DD vs DD/MM ambiguity.
5. **Overlay must live inside `buildVariantRows()`, not as a one-off step elsewhere.** Verified `buildSalesMaps()` has exactly one call site — index.html:8300 inside `buildVariantRows()` — and `_cachedSalesMaps` gets reset to `null` (forcing a full rebuild) from a dozen places across the app (every CSV reload, every manual refresh, every reset). If the AM overlay were applied anywhere else as a one-off, any of those triggers would silently wipe it back to stale CSV data on the next rebuild. The overlay has to be applied *inside* `buildVariantRows()`, immediately after index.html:8301 reassigns `window._salesBySkuDay`, so it reapplies every single time the cache rebuilds, by construction. The slow async AM fetch itself stays decoupled — `syncAMSales()` populates a cached `window.amRecentSalesMap` once (same pattern as `amApiData`), and the synchronous overlay step inside `buildVariantRows()` just applies whatever's already there.
6. **Atomicity — commit-or-abstain per day, never merge a partial count.** Build each day's AM total fully in memory before touching `salesBySkuDay`; if a page fetch for that day fails or times out partway (AM's API has shown occasional slowness/JSON parse hiccups), discard that day's overlay entirely rather than committing an incomplete number over a day that already has good CSV data — falls back to existing CSV-derived behaviour for that day, never worse than today.
7. **COGS gap:** AM order lines don't carry the same "Cost of goods sold" figure the Shopify CSV does. For AM-sourced days, leave `cogs` at 0 rather than guessing — margin calculations already have a fallback chain (`brCost||amCost||salesCost`) that doesn't strictly depend on this per-day COGS value, so this is a safe simplification, not a blocker.
8. **Sanity-checked the "hybrid" premise:** pulled a real current sales CSV and confirmed its rolling window already reaches through *today* (latest date = today, 451 distinct days back to 2025-05-11) — the pipeline isn't stale by days, only by however many hours since its last 3-hourly refresh. So the AM overlay's real job is filling a few-hour gap, not backfilling missing days — lower risk than initially scoped.
9. **Verify:** compare a day's AM-derived total sold units per SKU against the same day's Shopify CSV figures (once the CSV catches up ~3 hours later) to confirm the two sources agree before relying on AM as primary.

### Also in Phase 2 — real per-unit sell price, prorated from the order-level discount

Same order fetch as above (step 1), processed a second way — no new API calls. Real per-unit price only exists at the order level in AM (see "discounts reach AM, just not per line item" below); this reconstructs it per line.

10. For each fetched order, after excluding non-product/non-taxable lines (same filter as step 2) and skipping cancelled/non-approved orders (mirrors the existing Shopify CSV principle of "only count positive quantities, ignore returns/refunds" — check `credit_status` and any line with `qty_cxl>0`): for each remaining taxable line, prorate the order's actual discount by that line's share of the order —
   `lineDiscount = order.amount_discount × (line.amount / order.amount_subtotal)`
   `realUnitPrice = (line.amount − lineDiscount) / line.qty`
   Guard divide-by-zero (`amount_subtotal` should never be 0 for a real order, but skip rather than crash if it is). Verified against a real order (162075, 28.99% discount): correctly resolves $56.01 booked → $39.77 actually paid.
   Prorate by `amount_discount` (the resolved dollar figure) rather than reapplying `pct_discount` directly — handles the (unconfirmed but possible) case of a discount that doesn't apply uniformly across every line, and stays correct even if `override_discount_amount` was used instead of a straight percentage.
11. Aggregate **quantity-weighted**, not a naive average of averages: `avgRealPrice[sku] = Σ(realUnitPrice × qty) / Σ(qty)` across all matching lines for that SKU within the fetch window (same trailing window as the units-sold sync). A SKU with no AM order lines in the window has no entry — falls through to existing pricing.
12. Feed this into `priceMap[sku].price` in `buildSalesMaps()`, following the exact same **AM-preferred, existing-as-fallback** principle as everything else in this plan — when `avgRealPrice[sku]` exists, prefer it over today's `plEntry.price`/`pm.price`; when it doesn't (e.g. a slow-mover with no orders in the trailing window), today's logic is untouched. Computed and applied in the same pass, alongside the units-sold overlay, inside the same safe `buildVariantRows()` insertion point (step 5 above) — same atomicity guarantee (step 6): a day/order that failed mid-fetch contributes to neither the qty nor the price overlay.
13. **RRP/`compareAt` is not touched** — that's a solved problem via the existing Better-Reports/Shopify-sourced `compareAt`, and isn't what AM's order data helps with. Only the actual current *sell* price improves. Disc% (`1-price/compareAt`) automatically becomes more accurate as a side effect, no separate work needed.
14. **Verify:** for a handful of recently-discounted SKUs, confirm `avgRealPrice` matches hand-calculated proration (reuse the order-162075 style of check), confirm margin/Disc% update accordingly, and confirm a slow-moving SKU with no recent AM orders still shows today's existing price unchanged.

### Also in Phase 2 — Iconic marketplace commission (25% cut, WNDRR nets 75%)

Brendan flagged that Iconic Online Sales come through AM at full listed price, but Iconic (a marketplace) takes a 25% commission — so treating that price as WNDRR's real revenue overstates margin for every Iconic-sourced sale. Critically: **the AM customer gets changed to "ICONIC ONLINE SALES" manually, after the order already exists** — so filtering by `customer_name` alone would miss any Iconic order that hasn't been recoded yet at the moment the app happens to sync.

**Verified a reliable identifier that doesn't depend on the manual recode's timing**: every genuine WNDRR-direct order's `customer_po` is `#`-prefixed and short (`#144077`); every Iconic order's `customer_po` is a long numeric string with no `#` (confirmed against all 3 examples Brendan gave — `2639372172`, `2863272172`, `2769782172` — all matched exactly, including one still showing under `customer_name=ONLINE SALES`-shaped format when queried by PO directly). Detect Iconic orders by this **pattern on `customer_po`** (e.g. `/^\d{8,12}$/`, no `#`) applied to *every* fetched order regardless of which `customer_name` it currently sits under — this is correct the moment the order lands, independent of when (or whether yet) someone manually recodes it.

15. Also confirmed Iconic orders genuinely show `amount_discount: 0` / `pct_discount: 0` (all 3 examples) — "full priced" as described. The discount-proration logic from steps 10-11 still runs unchanged and correctly no-ops for these (no discount to prorate), so no special-casing needed there.
16. For any line identified as Iconic (by the `customer_po` pattern): after discount-proration (step 10) and converting to ex-GST (`/1.1`, matching the app's standing convention for margin), **apply the 25% commission on the ex-GST amount**: `netExGst = (realUnitPrice / 1.1) × 0.75` — confirmed with Brendan the cut comes off the ex-GST figure, not the GST-inclusive one.
17. Brendan wants this net-of-commission figure to show **everywhere** (Price, Sell Value, Margin%), not just margin. **Proposed mechanic, flagged for confirmation since it's an interpretation, not explicitly specified:** every other product's `Price` column is stored/shown GST-inclusive — to keep that column meaning the same thing on every row (rather than some rows being inc-GST and Iconic rows silently being ex-GST-net, which would make Price incomparable across products), re-gross the net figure back to an inc-GST-equivalent before it lands in `priceMap[sku].price`: `displayPrice = netExGst × 1.1`. So an Iconic sale that listed at $99.95 (ex-GST $90.86, net-of-commission $68.14) would show as **$74.96** in Price/Sell Value — genuinely what WNDRR's share is worth GST-inclusive, consistent with how every other product's Price is read, rather than the $99.95 marketplace listing figure. Margin is computed from the same `netExGst` figure as usual. **If this re-grossing isn't what's intended — e.g. Price should show the raw net-ex-GST number even though it won't match other rows' convention — confirm before implementing.**
18. For a SKU whose recent order lines are a mix of Iconic and direct-WNDRR sales, blend proportionally within the same quantity-weighted average from step 11 — each line contributes its own (commission-adjusted where applicable) net price, weighted by its own qty, same formula as before.
19. **Verify:** confirm the 3 example order numbers are correctly detected as Iconic regardless of their current `customer_name`, confirm the commission math against a hand-calculation for a real Iconic order, and confirm a non-Iconic sale for the same SKU in the same window is unaffected (full price, no commission deduction).

## Investigated separately — cost, ex-GST sell price & margin: does AM discount-blindness affect them?

Brendan suspected Shopify discounts might not flow through to Apparel Magic and asked for a precision check before locking in the scope boundary above (price/cost/margin staying Shopify/Better-Reports-sourced, not migrated to AM). Verified directly against real, currently-active discounts:

- **Confirmed true: AM's own `price`/`retail_price` fields do not reflect live Shopify discounts.** Pulled 4 products actually selling at 30–50% off right now (e.g. Oscar Hood Sweat, selling at $54.98 vs its $109.95 RRP) and queried their AM product records directly — AM shows the full, undiscounted RRP for all of them, `last_modified_time` weeks-to-months old, clearly untouched by the current sale. This is a static/wholesale-oriented field in AM, not something that updates when Shopify runs a promotion.
- **But this does not currently corrupt margin/price for live, already-selling products** — traced the exact priority order in `processRows()`: Better Reports' price list (`plMap`, fed by the Pipeline's "All Products" report) is checked *first*, and it **does** correctly reflect the live discounted price ($54.98, matching the real sale, confirmed against the same products) — this is Shopify-sourced data, not AM's. Only when no Better Reports entry exists does the code fall back to the most recent actual transaction price (`pm.price`, from real sales — also inherently discount-accurate, since it's literally what was charged). AM's price data is never consulted for a product that's already selling.
- **AM's (discount-blind) RRP is only ever used for genuinely pre-launch products** — zero stock, zero sales, not yet reflected in any Shopify price feed. Two code paths hit this: the AM-only "not yet in Shopify at all" branch in `processRows()` (already fixed in v2.2.47 to use RRP, not AM's wholesale `price` field) and a second fallback in `buildSalesMaps()` (index.html:7408-7432, for products that exist in Shopify inventory but haven't sold yet) — checked this one too, and it was already correctly preferring RRP over wholesale price even before that fix. Both are an inherent limitation, not a bug: a product with no live pricing data anywhere can't know about a discount that doesn't exist yet from its perspective. RRP is the best available estimate until the product actually syncs/sells.
- **Cost is unaffected by discounts entirely** — `amCost` comes from actual purchase-order amount/qty or `cost_landed`, a wholesale/landed-cost concept completely separate from retail markdowns. Discounting the sell price doesn't change what was paid to manufacture/land the product.

**Conclusion: the *static AM product record* (`price`/`retail_price` fields) should never feed price/margin — confirmed discount-blind, and today's Better-Reports-first chain already handles live products correctly without it.** That part of the scope boundary stays firm; no change there. What *does* change the picture is AM's real order *transaction* data, covered next — genuinely more accurate than a static record once properly reconstructed, and now included in Phase 2.

### Correction — discounts DO reach AM, just not per line item

Brendan spotted this by looking at a real AM order screen directly (order 162075) — its "Amounts" panel showed a genuine $49.86 discount. Pulled the same order's full raw data via the API to see exactly how that's structured, since it contradicts the *static product record* finding above (which is still correct, but is a different thing):

- **Order-level fields carry a real, accurate discount**: `amount_subtotal: 172.01`, `amount_discount: 49.86`, `pct_discount: 28.99%`, `amount(total): 146.97` — this is the real Shopify checkout discount, synced correctly.
- **But each `order_items` line is booked at full/list price, not the discounted price** — all 3 Soho Club Tee lines show `unit_price: 56.01` / `amount: 56.01` identically, and `56.01×3 + 3.98 (non-taxable return coverage) = 172.01`, exactly matching `amount_subtotal`. The 28.99% discount only exists as an order-level deduction; it's never distributed back down to the individual lines.
- So a single line item's `unit_price` is **not** a usable proxy for "what was actually paid per unit" — the real per-unit price only exists once you prorate the order-level discount across that order's taxable lines (excludes non-taxable service lines like return coverage/insurance, same exclusion list already needed for the units-sold work above). For this specific order, prorating gives $56.01 → **$39.77** actually paid per tee, not $56.01.

**Decision: pursue this — folded into Phase 2 above** (see "Also in Phase 2 — real per-unit sell price"). Genuinely more accurate than the current Better-Reports-first price for anything currently discounted, since it's the *actual* price of the *actual* transaction rather than a generic current-site-price snapshot. Reuses the exact same order fetch already needed for units-sold, so it's additive processing on data already being pulled, not a new API burden.

## Also in this update — retire the CLEARANCE status

Brendan confirmed the raw Shopify "Product vendor" CSV field is no longer being maintained, and the app has already superseded almost all of its former role with computed logic (CORE from the AM group, NEW/CURRENT/OLD from delivery-month age, REDUCED from real discount%, DRAFT/PRE-RELEASE from stock/status signals). Traced the one remaining genuine dependency: **CLEARANCE status is still driven purely by `vendor.toUpperCase()==='CLEARANCE'`** (index.html:8170) — nothing else auto-detects it. Since that field isn't being updated, no product can newly become CLEARANCE going forward; only pre-existing historically-tagged rows still show it.

Decision: retire CLEARANCE as a distinct status. A product that would have been manually tagged CLEARANCE should just fall through to the existing OLD classification (if old with nothing on order) or REDUCED (if it has an active discount) — exactly what already happens for every other product, no manual tagging required.

**Change is a clean removal, confirmed via full read of the surrounding block (index.html:8167-8200):**
- Delete the `isClearance` variable (index.html:8170).
- `isEnd`: drop the `||(isClearance||isBundle)` clause down to `||isBundle` (index.html:8173-8174).
- `isCurrent`: drop `&&!isClearance` (index.html:8180).
- `isNew`: drop `&&!isClearance` (index.html:8182).
- `effectiveVendor`: drop the `:isClearance?'CLEARANCE'` branch entirely (index.html:8191) — REDUCED already sits before this branch in the chain, so "OLD unless REDUCED" falls out naturally once CLEARANCE is removed, matching what was asked for.
- Reorder-flag exclusion: drop `isClearance` from `if(flag==='reorder'&&(isEnd||isClearance||isBundle))` (index.html:8200).
- **Leave the final `:vendor` fallback in `effectiveVendor` untouched** — that's a narrower, separate safety net for the rare case where a SKU's delivery month can't be decoded at all (so neither `isCurrent` nor `isEnd` can be determined), not part of what's being retired here.
